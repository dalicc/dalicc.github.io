Dependency Graph Viewer

Licenses do not conflict because two words differ; they conflict because the actions behind those words relate to each other. The DALICC reasoner reads that relationship from a dependency graph: 46 statements over the ODRL, Creative Commons and DALICC action vocabularies. That is what turns "this license permits odrl:sell" and "that license prohibits odrl:commercialize" into a reported conflict.

How reasoning works walks through what the reasoner does with these statements, step by step. To keep a graph of your own, with the statements you decide, use the Dependency Graph Editor.

Graphs you can reason with

  • DALICC deontic dependency graph core, default version 4

A core graph is curated by the project, and it is what everybody reasons with. While you are signed in, your own graphs and the ones shared with you appear here too, drafts included. Any of them can be chosen in the License Composer and the License Mixer straight away; manage your graphs.

History

The dependency graph has 4 versions. Version 4 is the one served today; every earlier version keeps its own address, so a conclusion drawn from one of them can still be checked against it.

  1. Version 4 current2026-09-23 Giray Havur

    The review of 2026-09-23 read every action the vocabulary defines against the axioms that mention it. Eight statements were added, three were removed, and the graph now holds 46. The commercial-use cluster is the substance of the change: the term 579 library statements use was not in the graph at all, so the axioms about selling and charging a fee concluded nothing.

    View Turtle

    11 changes

    • added cc:CommercialUse owl:sameAs odrl:commercialize

      dependency-graph-review cc:CommercialUse is 'income-generating use of any kind, whether direct or indirect' and odrl:commercialize is 'use the asset in a business environment', the reasoning counterpart of commercial use. 579 statements of the library name the first and none names the second, so the three axioms written about odrl:commercialize reached nothing. A license that permits charging a license fee now meets one that prohibits commercial use.

    • added odrl:commercialize owl:sameAs cc:CommercialUse

      dependency-graph-review The inverse spelling, written explicitly because the graph states every owl:sameAs pair in both directions.

    • added dalicc:sellCopy odrl:includedIn odrl:sell

      dependency-graph-review dalicc:sellCopy is 'the Assignee may sell the copies of the License Material' and odrl:sell is 'trade the asset for consideration', so selling copies is one way of selling. Through odrl:sell odrl:includedIn odrl:commercialize a prohibition of commercial use now reaches it.

    • added cc:DerivativeWorks odrl:includedIn odrl:distribute

      dependency-graph-review cc:DerivativeWorks is 'to distribute the derivative and making it available to the public', which is one kind of odrl:distribute, 'providing the work to the public or making it accessible to anyone else'. A license that forbids distribution now meets one that permits publishing a derivative.

    • added dalicc:ModifiedWorks odrl:includedIn odrl:distribute

      dependency-graph-review dalicc:ModifiedWorks is 'distributing a modified version of the work and making it available to the public', the counterpart of cc:DerivativeWorks for alterations that do not amount to a new work, and it is distribution for the same reason.

    • added odrl:AttachPolicy owl:sameAs cc:Notice

      dependency-graph-review The inverse of cc:Notice owl:sameAs odrl:AttachPolicy. The change log of version 3 says the graph states every owl:sameAs pair in both directions; three pairs did not, and now every one of them does.

    • added odrl:attachSource owl:sameAs cc:SourceCode

      dependency-graph-review The inverse of cc:SourceCode owl:sameAs odrl:attachSource, for the same reason.

    • added odrl:shareAlike owl:sameAs cc:ShareAlike

      dependency-graph-review The inverse of cc:ShareAlike owl:sameAs odrl:shareAlike, for the same reason.

    • removed odrl:extract owl:sameAs odrl:copy

      dependency-graph-review odrl:extract is 'extract part of the asset', which the term's own definition places inside odrl:reproduce rather than beside it. Calling it the same act as odrl:copy put extract, copy and reproduce in one equivalence class, so permitting the extraction of a part permitted copying the whole. odrl:extract odrl:includedIn odrl:reproduce carries the conclusions that do hold.

    • removed odrl:reproduce odrl:includedIn odrl:copy

      dependency-graph-review odrl:copy owl:sameAs odrl:reproduce already says the two names denote one act, so a subsumption between them states nothing and shows the pair twice with two different readings.

    • removed odrl:display odrl:includedIn odrl:play

      dependency-graph-review odrl:display is 'create a static and transient rendition of the work' and odrl:play is 'render the asset in an audio or video form'. A static rendition is not an audio or video one, so displaying is not a kind of playing. Both remain kinds of odrl:present.
